# Open Margin x Inspirit AI: optional pilot brief

## Positioning

Open Margin should be presented as an optional, independent research-communication and publication pathway that can sit after appropriate Inspirit AI projects. It does not replace coursework, mentorship, research supervision, a final presentation, a science fair, or an existing publication route.

The most credible initial fit includes humanities, social-sciences, and STEM work connected to AI, including ethics, policy, education, labor, media, law, history, culture, data, computing, and other social or technical impacts of technology. A technical machine-learning research pathway should not be promised unless Open Margin has the qualified reviewers and advisors to assess it responsibly.

## Why the partnership can help

- Students get an optional chance to revise a finished project or paper for an external audience, respond to structured feedback, and practice communicating research beyond a demo or presentation.
- Inspirit AI gains a no-cost, post-program pathway that can extend a strong final project without claiming that every student will publish.
- Open Margin gains a better prepared, opt-in group of students whose work has already had meaningful research support.
- Both organizations protect student trust by keeping participation voluntary, editorial review independent, and publication decisions separate from program enrollment or payment.

## Ten creation steps completed

1. Aligned the submission guide, form, and backend around a 2,500 to 8,000 word PDF or DOCX submission contract.
2. Removed illustrative article titles so the pilot issue never implies that unpublished work exists.
3. Added a public author toolkit with a concrete manuscript-readiness self-check.
4. Added a privacy-preserving submission-status lookup using the submission reference and original email.
5. Added a reviewer and advisor recruitment page that explains standards, capacity, and conflicts of interest.
6. Added a secure reviewer-application portal with guardian safeguards for applicants ages 14 to 17.
7. Added a partner page that explains how a program can complement the journal without steering or guaranteeing publication.
8. Added a secure partner-inquiry portal for schools, research programs, and youth organizations.
9. Added an authorized recruitment desk where editors can review and move reviewer applications and partner inquiries through a pipeline.
10. Added private editorial email alerts for reviewer applications and partner inquiries, alongside the existing submission alert.

## Guardrails for any pilot

- No use of Inspirit AI's name, logo, or branding on the Open Margin site without written approval.
- No preferred review, priority, or guaranteed outcome for referred students.
- No fee for submission or publication.
- Students submit directly and opt in themselves.
- Student information remains separate unless a written, consent-based workflow is agreed.
- Start with a small, fitting cohort and review aggregate learnings after the pilot.

## Email draft

**Subject:** Exploring an optional publishing pathway for Inspirit AI students

Hi Jared,

I hope you are doing well. As someone who has worked with Inspirit AI, I have been thinking about a small way to extend the strong project and research work students already complete through the program.

I recently built Open Margin, an independent, free, student-led journal for rigorous student scholarship of any age across the humanities, social sciences, and STEM. I think it could complement Inspirit AI's existing programs through an optional pilot for students whose AI work has a strong research-communication, AI-and-society, or STEM-research component.

Your 1:1 research program already helps students develop a final research paper and explore journal or science-fair submission routes. Open Margin could offer a practical next step for appropriate projects: students would revise a paper for an external audience, receive structured editorial feedback, and, if accepted, publish an open-access piece they can include in a portfolio.

A small pilot could focus on topics such as AI ethics, policy, education, labor, media, law, data, computing, or other interdisciplinary questions connected to students' AI projects. Specialized technical work would be considered only when qualified reviewers are available. The structure would be intentionally lightweight:

- students opt in and submit directly;
- there is no cost to students;
- eligibility and research-integrity guidance are public;
- editorial review stays independent, with no guarantee of publication; and
- after the pilot, we can discuss aggregate learnings about readiness and feedback without sharing private student information.

I believe this gives students a meaningful way to carry strong work beyond a final presentation while giving Inspirit an optional, ethically grounded outcome layer. It does not replace your curriculum or mentorship. It gives eligible students another way to practice revision, research communication, and public scholarship.

Would you be open to a 20-minute conversation to explore whether a small opt-in pilot could make sense? I would be happy to walk you through the journal, the public author toolkit, and the safeguards around consent, privacy, and editorial independence.
