# Open Margin: 10-submission campaign

This is an acquisition plan for reaching **10 completed, good-fit submissions**
in the private editor desk. It is not a promise of 10 acceptances, 10
publications, or a fixed review time. The campaign runs only while the public
editorial record and the review bench can support new authors.

## The first gate is trust

Before new author outreach, reconcile the public issue with the private
editorial records.

1. For every public article, confirm a completed review record, a final edit,
   and author or guardian approval for the public version.
2. If a pilot article does not have that evidence, correct its public status
   after communicating with the author. Do not market the issue as a set of
   accepted papers until this is done.
3. Check that every public article opens in a readable format and can be
   downloaded when the site promises a downloadable paper.
4. Record the correction in the public updates or transparency record without
   naming the author or exposing private correspondence.

The current context contains a September 9 baseline of four submissions, three
tagged submissions, one submission in screening, and one reviewer application.
That baseline is stale and private. Verify the current count in `/editor` on
the first day, then calculate `10 - current completed submissions` as the
remaining target. The three public articles are publication evidence, not a
substitute for the submission count.

## What counts

Count one submission when the portal has received a manuscript and the private
editor desk shows the record. Do not count a click, a reply, a partner promise,
an abandoned form, a duplicate upload, or a paper that was sent outside the
portal.

Keep these measures separate:

| Measure | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Completed submissions | Primary progress toward 10. |
| Good-fit submissions | Checks whether the message reaches the right authors. |
| Tagged submissions | Shows which public referral path was used. |
| Reviewer applications | Protects the review promise as volume grows. |
| Partner replies and inquiries | Shows whether a program will share the option. |
| First decisions sent | Shows whether the workflow is keeping up. |
| Articles published | Public record only; never use it to inflate the submission count. |

## The acquisition system

Use several small, attributable paths instead of one large blast.

| Audience | Asset | Link | Desired action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AP, IB, and school-research authors | AP/IB fit page | `/submit/ap-research?utm_source=school_research&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=fall-2026` | A student reads the requirements and submits directly if ready. |
| Teachers, librarians, and mentors | Class-project fit page | `/submit/class-project?utm_source=teacher&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=fall-2026` | An adult shares the public page when a student asks where to send work. |
| Data and methods communities | Research-note fit page | `/submit/research-note?utm_source=research&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=fall-2026` | A student with a public-data project checks fit and submits a research note. |
| Research programs and warm referrers | Forwardable share page | `/share?utm_source=program&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=fall-2026` | A program reviews the process and shares it optionally. |
| Moderator-approved communities | Publishing guide | `/guide?utm_source=community&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=fall-2026` | Readers find an honest answer without a disguised advertisement. |

The site records only the optional campaign source, medium, campaign name, and
landing path with a submission. It does not track IP addresses, device IDs, or
browsing history. Keep the public page identical for every author.

## Four-week schedule

### September 16 to 18: repair and baseline

- Reconcile public articles, submission statuses, reviews, final edits, and
  author approvals.
- Fix any unreadable public file or misleading status before inviting new
  authors.
- Record the exact current submission, reviewer, partner, and decision counts
  in the private marketing desk.
- Publish the three route pages only after the site owner approves the copy and
  the build passes.

### September 19 to 25: warm distribution

- Follow up with people who already agreed to share Open Margin, using the
  public share page and the corrected record.
- Send at most five individually tailored messages to public organizational
  addresses. Ask for a process review or optional resource share, not student
  names or an endorsement.
- Invite up to three qualified adult reviewers using the reviewer pack. Keep
  author acquisition paused if one qualified independent reviewer plus a backup are not available
  for the fields being promoted.
- Record each message, link, date, reply, and next action in a private log.

### September 26 to October 2: permission-based expansion

- Send one follow-up only to contacts who have not opted out, after at least
  five business days.
- Send a second batch of at most five public organizational messages only if
  the first batch did not reveal a trust, fit, or capacity problem.
- Ask one moderator or community owner for permission before posting the
  two-paragraph guide message.
- Answer author questions with the public review, policy, and toolkit pages.

### October 3 to 9: improve conversion, not pressure

- Compare route-level submissions and eligibility. Keep the route that brings
  clear, complete work; revise or pause a route that brings repeated off-scope
  files.
- Add the most common genuine author question to the relevant public page.
- Offer a short public checklist or office-hours explanation only where the
  host permits it. Never review a private paper in a public thread.
- Do not expand outreach if decisions or reviewer assignments are falling
  behind.

### October 10 to 16: close the loop

- Stop new author outreach at 10 completed submissions or at the safe capacity
  chosen by the editor, whichever comes first.
- Send no more than one final close-the-loop note to a contact who requested
  information and has not opted out.
- Record the cumulative total, good-fit rate, tagged-source mix, reviewer
  capacity, partner replies, and first-decision status.
- Choose three actions for the next month: continue, change, and stop.

## Message rules

Every message should contain the same essential facts: free to submit, authors
of any age, humanities/social sciences/STEM scope, direct submission, public
review process, one independent written review for eligible work, no guarantee of
acceptance, and the relevant privacy or guardian rule.

Use the route that matches the reader:

**For AP, IB, or school research**

> If you have a finished AP Research project, IB Extended Essay, or similar
> school paper, Open Margin is one optional place to compare. Read the fit page
> first: it explains the manuscript requirements, double-blind file, guardian
> approval, and the fact that publication is not guaranteed.

**For a teacher, mentor, or librarian**

> Some students finish a class, capstone, science-fair, or lab project and ask
> where it can go next. This public checklist is an optional resource. Students
> decide whether to submit, and the program does not send us names or papers.

**For a public-data project**

> Open Margin has a research-note path for a 1,500 to 3,000 word paper built
> from data anyone can download. The dataset link is required, a code link is
> optional, and the author should explain the method and limits.

Do not use scarcity, prestige, admissions language, guaranteed outcomes, fake
testimonials, or a claim that Open Margin is indexed or accredited. Do not buy
lists, scrape student addresses, or send bulk unsolicited email.

## Stop rules

- Pause all new author acquisition while a public article has an unresolved
  status, approval, review, or readability mismatch.
- Pause a channel after repeated off-scope submissions, requests for special
  treatment, or pressure on minors.
- Do not promise a six-week decision when the reviewer bench cannot support it.
- Do not ask partners to forward student data or manuscripts.
- Do not send a second message to a contact who asked not to be contacted.
- Do not trade submission volume for weaker screening, missing disclosures, or
  no completed independent written review.
