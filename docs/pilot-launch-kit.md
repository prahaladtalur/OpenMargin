# Open Margin pilot launch kit

Use this kit to run a small, responsible first-call pilot. The goal is to learn whether the editorial workflow, reviewer capacity, and author experience work well before a wider launch.

## Pilot target

- Invite 10 to 20 students to submit.
- Recruit 3 to 5 adult academic advisors, educators, or research-communication volunteers.
- Aim to accept and publish 3 to 5 finished pieces for the first issue.
- Keep the pilot free for every author and contributor.
- Do not promise review speed, acceptance, publication, or special treatment.

## Before sending invitations

- [ ] Confirm that at least two editors can monitor the submission desk weekly.
- [ ] Confirm the notification inbox can receive messages.
- [ ] Identify an adult advisor who can help with difficult editorial, safeguarding, or conflict-of-interest questions.
- [ ] Read the public policies and review process so every response is consistent.
- [ ] Decide who will reply to applications and submissions within five business days.
- [ ] Test one non-sensitive submission and its status lookup, then remove that test record from the editor desk.
- [ ] Keep a private list of invited students and contacts. Do not add students to a mailing list without consent.

## Recommended two-week sequence

### Days 1 and 2: recruit reviewer capacity

Send the reviewer outreach below to teachers, graduate students, researchers, librarians, journalists, and other adults with relevant subject expertise. Screen applicants for field fit, availability, and conflicts before assigning any manuscript.

### Days 3 and 4: invite a small author cohort

Send the student invitation to students with work that fits the journal's humanities, social-sciences, or STEM scope. Share the public submission page, not a private form, so every author sees the same requirements.

### Days 5 through 10: acknowledge and screen submissions

Use the editor desk to check fit, anonymization, file format, author eligibility, and guardian requirements. Ask authors for missing information before sending work to review.

### Days 11 through 14: calibrate and assign reviews

Give reviewers the public rubric and a sample review first. Assign only manuscripts that match their field and have no disclosed conflict. Keep communication constructive and specific.

Hi [First name],

I am helping launch Open Margin, a free, student-led journal for rigorous secondary-school scholarship across the humanities, social sciences, and STEM. We are preparing a small pilot issue and are looking for a few thoughtful adult advisors and reviewers.

Contributors help us give students useful, ethical feedback. Depending on your expertise and availability, the role may involve a calibration exercise, occasional blinded manuscript review, subject-specific guidance, citation checks, or editorial consultation. We do not ask reviewers to take on work outside their field or capacity.

We are especially interested in people with experience in [relevant field], research communication, teaching, libraries, journalism, or youth scholarship. Every contributor agrees to confidentiality, conflict disclosure, and constructive feedback.

## Launch-day checklist

- [ ] Confirm the live links for `/submit`, `/resources`, `/reviewers`, `/status`, and `/policies` work.
- [ ] Send reviewer outreach first, then author invitations after capacity is confirmed.
- [ ] Record the date, audience, and exact copy used for each invitation.
- [ ] Check the editor desk and notification inbox on the first day after sending invitations.
- [ ] Reply to every applicant or author with a clear next step, even when the answer is no.
- [ ] Review pilot results before inviting a larger cohort: submissions received, turnaround time, reviewer workload, author questions, and any policy gaps.
