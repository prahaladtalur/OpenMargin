# Open Margin: 30-day marketing sprint

This plan is designed for a small, student-led journal. It favors useful links,
clear claims, and direct referrals over broad promotion. Do not send a message,
post in a community, or add a contact to a list without permission.

## The goal

Bring in a small number of good-fit submissions and learn which people help
authors find the journal. The primary measure is completed, eligible
submissions. Impressions and follower counts are secondary.

## The operating numbers

Record these numbers each Monday in the private marketing desk:

| Measure | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Completed submissions | Shows whether the portal is usable. |
| Tagged submissions | Shows whether a referral link was used. |
| Eligible submissions | Shows whether the audience understands the scope. |
| Reviewer applications | Protects the review timeline. |
| Partner replies | Shows whether a program sees a useful fit. |
| First decisions sent | Shows whether growth is safe for current capacity. |
| Articles published | Shows whether outreach becomes a public record. |

Do not collect IP addresses, device IDs, or browsing history for this plan. The
submission form records only an optional campaign source, medium, campaign name,
and landing path. The private marketing desk groups those labels after a
submission.

## Link naming

Use lowercase labels. Keep the campaign name the same for the whole month.

```text
Campaign: fall-2026
Source: partner, teacher, program, reddit, linkedin, email
Medium: referral, community, social, outreach
```

Examples:

```text
https://openmargin.org/guide?utm_source=partner&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=fall-2026
https://openmargin.org/submit?utm_source=teacher&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=fall-2026
https://openmargin.org/share?utm_source=program&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=fall-2026
https://openmargin.org/guide?utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=fall-2026
```

Give one organization one link. Do not use a private or hidden link. The public
page must show the same fees, review process, privacy policy, and limits for
every author.

## 30-day calendar

### Days 1 to 3: make the path easy to trust

- Read `/guide`, `/review`, `/resources`, `/policies`, and `/share` as a first-time author.
- Check every link on the submission path on a phone and a laptop.
- Keep the short introduction video, the public review process, and the public record easy to find.
- Confirm the editor notification inbox and the submission status flow with one safe test.
- Open `/editor/marketing` and record the baseline. Do not count the safe test as a real lead.

### Days 4 to 7: prepare the referral kit

- Choose three audiences: research programs, teachers or librarians, and adult reviewers.
- Give each audience one UTM link and one short message from `docs/marketing-copy-pack.md`.
- Ask one trusted adult to check the claims, safeguarding language, and tone.
- Prepare a plain-text version for email and a two-sentence version for a chat or Slack channel.
- Do not ask an organization to send student names or papers to Open Margin.

### Days 8 to 14: use warm, permission-based referrals

- Send or share only with people who know the sender or have a public contact route.
- Start with three tailored messages, one per audience. Wait at least five business days before one follow-up.
- Ask for criticism or a resource-list review, not an endorsement.
- Log the source, date, audience, and exact link in a private note.
- Reply to questions with the public policy page. Do not promise acceptance, admissions value, or a fixed decision date.

### Days 15 to 21: publish useful answers

- Share the guide page when someone asks where to publish a student paper.
- Publish one short answer from the guide in a place that allows self-promotion. State that you built Open Margin.
- Share the author toolkit with teachers and mentors who ask how to prepare a manuscript.
- Invite qualified adults to the reviewer page before inviting more authors.
- Check the marketing desk. If a source produces poor-fit work, stop that source and revise the message.

### Days 22 to 26: improve what authors see

- Review the most common author question and add the answer to the public guide or policies.
- Check that the submission page explains the scope, file requirements, AI disclosure, and guardian rules before upload.
- Add one real published article or a clear pilot-issue update when the editorial workflow supports it.
- Ask a recent author, if one exists, for permission to use a short factual testimonial. Never publish private feedback without consent.

### Days 27 to 30: review and choose the next month

- Compare tagged and untagged submissions, eligibility, reviewer capacity, and reply time.
- Keep only channels that bring good-fit work or useful partner feedback.
- Write three decisions: continue, change, and stop.
- Set a safe submission target for the next month based on reviewer capacity.
- Update this file with the result. Do not expand outreach until the review timeline is stable.

## Stop rules

- Stop a channel if it brings repeated off-scope work, pressure on minors, or requests for special treatment.
- Stop invitations if two qualified reviewers are not available for the fields shown as open.
- Do not use paid ads until the free referral path works and the review capacity is known.
- Do not buy student contact lists or scrape personal email addresses.
- Do not claim indexing, prestige, selectivity, or admissions benefit that Open Margin cannot prove.

## End-of-month review

Write one short note with:

1. The number of completed and eligible submissions.
2. The sources that produced them.
3. The number of reviewer and partner replies.
4. The median time to the first author reply.
5. One public page that needs a change.
6. The next month's safe capacity.
